The basics

What the waist to height ratio tells you

WHtR compares the circumference of your waist with your height. Fat stored around the abdomen – especially deep around the organs – is linked more closely with type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease than fat stored elsewhere. Because the ratio is scaled to your height, it works for tall and short people alike.

In 2022 the UK’s National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) added the ratio to its guidance and summed it up in one easy message: keep your waist to less than half your height.

The formula
WHtR = waist ÷ height
Example80 cm ÷ 170 cm = 0.47
Imperial32 in ÷ 67 in = 0.48

No calculator? No problem

The string test – and how to measure

Cut a piece of string to your height, fold it in half and wrap it around your waist. If the ends meet or overlap, your ratio is below 0.5. It is the simplest health check you can do at home.

  • Find the spot. Locate your lowest rib and the top of your hip bone – the waist sits halfway between them.
  • Stand relaxed. Feet together, arms loose, and breathe out normally before reading the tape.
  • Keep it level. The tape should be snug but not pressing into the skin, and parallel to the floor.
  • Measure height barefoot. Stand against a wall and mark the top of your head with a flat object.

FAQ

Waist to height ratio – your questions

What is a good waist to height ratio?

For most adults a waist-to-height ratio between 0.40 and 0.49 is associated with no increased health risk. The simple rule from NICE guidance is to keep your waist to less than half your height.

How do I calculate waist to height ratio?

Divide your waist measurement by your height, using the same unit for both. A waist of 80 cm and a height of 170 cm give 80 ÷ 170 = 0.47.

Is waist to height ratio better than BMI?

WHtR reflects fat stored around the middle, which BMI cannot show. That is why NICE recommends using it alongside BMI for adults with a BMI under 35. Neither measure is a diagnosis on its own.

Is the ratio different for men and women?

No. The same boundaries of 0.5 and 0.6 are used for adult men and women, which makes the ratio easy to remember.

Can children use the waist to height ratio?

Researchers often apply the 0.5 boundary from around age five, but results for children and teenagers should always be discussed with a paediatrician or GP rather than judged at home.

What does a ratio below 0.4 mean?

A ratio under 0.40 is not a NICE risk band, but it can go along with low body weight. If your result is in this range, it is worth checking your overall health with a doctor.
